Campus P2P content distribution

The file your student needs is already three doors down.

Campvus lets enrolled coursemates share lecture slides, readings, and past papers directly with each other over Wi-Fi first — so the same file is only ever downloaded from the school once, not once per student.

Nonprofit & MIT-licensed. Self-hostable, built for schools with real bandwidth constraints — not a SaaS trial.

How one download becomes forty

  • Peer hop — free, local
  • Also enrolled, not needed yet

Why it matters

One paid download. Everyone else, free.

Say a lecturer posts a 25 MB slide deck, and 40 students in the same residence all need it before their 9am class.

Without Campvus1,000 MB

40 students × 25 MB, each pulled from the school's server

With Campvus25 MB

1 download from the server, 39 peer hops over Wi-Fi

Under the hood

Every file is addressed, signed, and verified — never just trusted.

01

Upload

A teacher uploads a file, or Campvus detects one your LMS already received.

02

Hash & sign

The file is content-hashed and its manifest signed with the institution's key.

03

Announce

The signed manifest is published to that course's own private swarm topic.

04

Find a peer

Enrolled devices look nearby first — same Wi-Fi, then same area, then anywhere else enrolled.

05

Fall back

Nobody nearby yet? It downloads from the school's own storage, hash-verified on arrival.

Pick your integration

Two ways to run it.

Mode A

Beside your LMS

No new accounts, no new upload workflow. A tray-only desktop companion watches for files your LMS already has and distributes them peer-to-peer in the background — your LMS stays the system of record.

Mode B

The full app

No LMS yet? Campvus runs the whole thing — course rosters, uploads, storage, and enrollment — with peer-to-peer distribution built in from day one.

Nonprofit & open source

No vendor lock-in. No growth targets to hit.

Campvus is free, open-source infrastructure for schools with limited bandwidth — not a funded startup with a subscription to justify. Fork it, self-host it, or read the architecture before you trust it with real students' data.
